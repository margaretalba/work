The challenge

Stitch Fix is an online styling service, and Meta is where it finds new clients and wins back old ones. My work covers both: lower-funnel ads for people close to signing up, and reactivation ads for clients who drifted away. Each audience needs a different message, the women’s and men’s lines each need their own look, and all of it runs on a busy promo calendar.

Reactivation

Bringing lapsed clients back.

Reactivation ads reach people who’ve used Stitch Fix before and drifted away. They already know the service, so the job is giving them a good reason to come back. I created the ads for several reactivation campaigns, each with its own offer for a different group of lapsed clients: 10% off, a waived styling fee, 20% off two Fixes, or just what’s new.

My strongest statics became top performers, bringing lapsed clients back for less than the dynamic product ads. One got more budget and kept getting more efficient as it grew.

I also designed the frame for the product ads in a $40-off limited-time offer. Those ads became the top performers for both the women’s and men’s lines.

Start from what’s worked.

I start with the goal and the data. Past results tell me which layouts and offers work, and I build the next round from there.

Lead with the clothes.

Lapsed clients already know how Stitch Fix works, so the ad doesn’t need to explain it. I lead with exciting merch, picked with Lynette Pone, our visual content strategist. New clients get the opposite: brands and how it works.

Fresh, but familiar.

Lapsed clients have seen our ads before. I build on past winners and stay on brand, but change enough that each campaign feels new.

Acquisition

96 assets for one limited-time offer.

One offer, always visible.

New clients scroll fast, so the offer sits in the same place on every ad. One glance is enough.

Different hooks, same offer.

People stop scrolling for different reasons, so each ad leads with its own angle while the offer stays put. Copywriter Emily Saulnier and I paired every line with a visual built for it.

Speaking to each audience.

Women and men shop differently, so each line gets its own visual approach.

Keeping it on schedule.

Promos don’t wait. My work moves through reviews fast enough that every asset is ready by launch.

What I learned

An ad is a tiny space, so every part of it has to earn its place. I learned to start with the audience: what does a new client want, what would bring a lapsed client back, and what do they actually need to see? Then I build the creative around that.
