The challenge

Most of my promotions run everywhere at once. Each one has to reach women, men and parents across CRM, site, app, banners and social, with an offer that lands the same way everywhere.

How I run a promo
  1. 1
    Brief

    I go over the brief with my marketing partners and ask the questions that shape the work.

  2. 2
    Partners

    I talk with merchandising, growth and CRM about the products, the audiences and where each piece will run.

  3. 3
    Create

    I design the assets for every channel in Figma.

  4. 4
    Review and launch

    The work goes through two rounds of review, then launches.

  5. 5
    Learn

    Each month we go over what worked and what didn’t, and I apply those findings to the next round.

The approach
Let results set the details.

Past performance decides the specifics, like which banner height works best and where the CTA goes. Every month’s results feed the next round of promos. It shows: my short emails with a single animated hero and a clear offer were a top performer two months in a row.

Let the offer do the work.

A clearance shopper is deciding one thing: is the discount worth a look? So the offer is the biggest message in every piece, and the email is short enough that nothing competes with it.

Each channel has its own job.

Everything needs to look related, but each channel has its own strengths. Email and social have to stop someone mid-scroll, so they lead loud with the discount, and on social, movement and quick cuts do the work. The site reaches people who are already shopping, so there the offer fits into the experience instead of interrupting it.

Kids get their own look.

Kids is its own audience, so I designed those pieces separately and made them playful. The offer stays front and center, so it still reads as the same sale.

What I learned

A promo that runs everywhere has to look related, but it shouldn’t look the same everywhere. Playing to each channel’s strengths, and each audience’s, is what makes one offer work across all of them.
