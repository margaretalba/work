Stitch Fix Promotions

The challenge

Most of my promotions run everywhere at once. Each one has to reach women, men and parents across CRM, site, app, banners and social, with an offer that lands the same way everywhere.

The approach
Let the offer do the work.

A promo shopper is deciding one thing: is this offer worth a look? So the offer is the biggest message in every piece, and emails stay short enough that nothing competes with it.

Each channel has its own job.

Everything needs to look related, but each channel has its own strengths.

ChannelIts jobWhat I optimize
CRMGet the clickAn offer-first hero, with the CTA above the fold
Site and appConvert shoppers who are already browsingBanner height and where the offer sits
SocialStop the scrollMovement and quick cuts
KidsReach parents, a different audienceA separate, playful look with the same offer
Kids get their own look.

Kids is its own audience, so I designed those pieces separately and made them playful. The offer stays front and center, so it still reads as the same sale.

How I improve results

Each month, the team reviews what worked and what didn’t, and I take those findings into the next round. Two that changed how I build promos:

  • Banner height.

    Shorter banners perform better, but only to a point. Too short or too tall, and results drop.

  • CTA placement.

    The CTA goes above the fold, so no one has to scroll to act.

Results

  • A clearance email I designed was a top performer for both the women’s and men’s lines, and the team made its layout the template for future clearance promos.

  • A 40%-off Instagram post was one of the month’s top two for women, with engagement well above the monthly average.

  • Short emails with a single animated hero and a clear offer were a top performer two months in a row.

What I learned

A promo that runs everywhere has to look related, but it shouldn’t look the same everywhere. Playing to each channel’s strengths, and each audience’s, is what makes one offer work across all of them.
