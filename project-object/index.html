Project Object

I designed the brand, ran the events and designed the online shop for a new Portland store that gave back to its community.

The challenge

Project Object opened on NE Sandy Boulevard in Portland in April 2017, a new independent shop with a clear point of view. Its owner, T Ngu, also runs the jewelry line Upper Metal Class. The shop stocked work from women-owned brands, artists of color and LGBTQ designers, and committed 10% of its profits to giving back. A brand-new store had to become a place people knew and trusted, in person and online.

Details on the shop’s opening, mission and giving come from Portland Monthly.

The approach
One logo, everywhere.

I designed the store’s identity and marketing materials around a single wordmark. The same high-contrast serif logo is on the front door, the sign, the opening poster, postcards and emails, so customers saw the same brand on the street, on paper and in their inbox.

Photoshoots.

I scouted locations and worked with models, photographers and stylists on photoshoots for the product pages and social media.

Events.

I planned and ran fundraisers and community gatherings with artists, Planned Parenthood and Django Girls. They fit what the shop stood for and gave people a reason to come in beyond shopping. I promoted them on Facebook, Instagram and Eventbrite.

The website.

I designed the Shopify site and photographed the products for it, so customers who couldn’t visit still got the shop’s point of view.

What I learned

At a shop this size, the brand is everything people run into: the sign, the poster, the events, the emails, the site. I tried to make them all feel like the same room.
